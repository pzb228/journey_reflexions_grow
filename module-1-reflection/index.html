Module 1 - Reflection

Module 1 challenged me to rethink education beyond traditional classroom practices and academic outcomes. Through Indigenous perspectives, land-based learning, storytelling, and reflection, I began to understand teaching as something deeply connected to relationships, community, identity, and the environment. The following takeaways represent the learnings that impacted me the most and that I hope to meaningfully integrate into my future teaching practice.

TAKEAWAY 1: Learning From the LandTAKEAWAY 2: First Peoples Principles of LearningTAKEAWAY 3: Meaningful Land Acknowledgements

What I learned: Working outside the classroom is deeply meaningful because it generates learning that truly lasts over time and can be naturally related to our daily lives.

Why it was important for me: It led me to question traditional education quite deeply. We shouldn't focus solely on academic content, but also on our relationship with the environment. If we value the planet and build respectful relationships with it, we will be rewarded with its abundance.

How I will apply it to my teaching practice: I will design more outdoor learning experiences, prioritize observing nature, connect scientific ideas with Indigenous knowledge, value the importance of every living being, and foster a deep sense of respect and awareness.

What I learned: Learning is inherently connected to relationships, identity, community, and reflection. When we truly internalize the meaning of this, our relationships with the environment flow naturally, without us having to force our actions.

Why it was important for me: It generated a genuine paradigm shift in my teaching approach, making it much more human, holistic, and heartfelt.

How I will apply it to my teaching practice: I believe my reflections reflect my deepest thoughts, so publishing them in my journal can serve as practical tools for other educators. Likewise, in the classroom, I will actively try to integrate students and their families to strengthen our community ties.

What I learned: Making a Land Acknowledgement cannot be just a routine or a checklist; it must be done consciously and reflectively. As educators, we teach based on our emotions, and when something isn't truly meaningful to us, we don't put the same passion into it—and students notice immediately. Therefore, we must love and respect this content first.

Why it was important for me: It helped me understand the profound relationship between territory, history, and our shared responsibility. We cannot settle for just one version of history; we must go further and respect different points of view.

How I will apply it to my teaching practice: I will investigate local territories alongside my students to co-create authentic acknowledgments. I will make sure that the depth and framing of these discussions are always appropriate to the students' developmental and chronological age.

This module helped me rethink education as something deeply connected to relationships, community, identity, and the land. It also reminded me that reconciliation is not just about learning history, but about transforming the way we teach, listen, and build respectful learning environments. I hope to continue discovering how to integrate Indigenous perspectives in meaningful and authentic ways throughout my teaching practice.
