Module - 6 - Reflection

This module really pushed me to deepen my understanding of Indigenous education and challenge my own assumptions about creating inclusive, culturally responsive classrooms. Between the readings, videos, and discussions, I walked away with a much clearer, more nuanced grasp of decolonization, Indigenization, authentic resources, assessment, and cultural safety.

Artifacts and Key Takeaways

  • Decolonization vs. Indigenization Comparison Chart: Creating this chart was a turning point for me. It helped me realize that while both concepts work toward transforming education, they aren't interchangeable. Decolonization is about actively questioning and dismantling colonial structures, whereas Indigenization focuses on weaving Indigenous ways of knowing, learning, and being into our educational spaces. This activity made me look much closer at the language I use and the actual intentions behind my teaching.
  • Summary and Reflection on Potlatch as Pedagogy: This piece highlighted just how vital relationships, reciprocity, storytelling, and community are to the learning process. It was a great reminder that meaningful learning is rooted in experience and connection. As an elementary teacher, I can bring this into my classroom by making more room for collaborative learning, oral storytelling, and genuine community involvement.
  • Annotated Bibliography of Authentic Indigenous Resources: This task drove home the responsibility we have to choose materials created directly by Indigenous authors, organizations, and Knowledge Keepers. I learned that authenticity takes careful vetting—just because a resource claims to represent an Indigenous perspective doesn't mean it’s accurate or respectful. Moving forward, this will guide how I curate classroom materials to ensure Indigenous voices are represented properly.
  • Assessment Tool Designed Using Indigenous Frameworks: This was easily one of the most practical assignments in the module because it forced me to rethink assessment beyond traditional academic metrics. I explored ways to center student voice, self-reflection, relationships, and holistic growth. Going forward, I want to keep designing assessments that value different ways of showing learning and celebrate the unique strengths of every student.
  • Reflection on Cultural Safety: The resources and discussions surrounding this topic made me hyper-aware of how deeply systemic, ongoing colonial practices still impact Indigenous students and their families today. I spent a lot of time reflecting on actionable strategies: building trust, weaving Indigenous perspectives naturally throughout the entire school year, and fostering a classroom environment where everyone feels safe and actually belongs. These are practices I am committed to strengthening.

Conclusion and Inquiry Connection

This module ties directly into my inquiry project by providing concrete, practical strategies to build a learning environment that genuinely respects and responds to Indigenous perspectives. It has also reinforced the idea that this work requires continuous learning and self-reflection.

Blending my past teaching experience with everything I’ve learned over these first six modules, I feel incredibly well-equipped to integrate Indigenous perspectives meaningfully—not as isolated, check-the-box activities, but as an organic part of my practice. My ultimate goal is to keep building authentic relationships, sourcing proper materials, and creating learning experiences that honor Indigenous knowledge while supporting every single learner in my room
