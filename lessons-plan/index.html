The Salmon's Journey

Activity Description:

In this unit, Grade 1 and 2 students explore the interconnected life cycle of the local Pacific Salmon. Rather than studying the salmon in isolation, students engage in place-based learning by mapping the salmon's journey from local freshwater creeks to the ocean and back. The core of the activity involves outdoor trail walks along nearby streams to observe the environment, alongside storytelling sessions that root the scientific life stages—from egg and alevin to spawner—in Indigenous oral traditions.

Connecting to the Local Life Cycle & Ecosystem Respect:

Through this exploration, students develop a deep relational understanding of the ecosystem, realizing that the salmon's return sustains the entire local environment, including the temperate rainforest, wildlife, and community. By embedding the First Peoples Principles of Learning—specifically that learning is holistic, reflexive, and experiential—students shift from simply learning about nature to learning in relation with nature. This builds an organic sense of stewardship, empathy, and deep respect for the water and all living relatives.

Trees as Our Teachers

Activity Description:

This project transforms the forest and natural areas surrounding the school into our primary classroom. Students participate in regular, intentional land-based learning walks to build a personal relationship with the local tree species (such as the Western Red Cedar and Douglas Fir). Students engage their physical senses to observe seasonal changes, touch the bark, listen to the wind in the leaves, and gather fallen natural materials for classroom learning.

Interacting with the Natural Environment:

By treating trees not as mere resources but as "teachers" and knowledge keepers, students practice Two-Eyed Seeing (Etuaptmumk). They learn to balance Western scientific observations (identifying leaf patterns, bark textures, and seasonal cycles) with Indigenous perspectives of kinship and reciprocal responsibility. This practice honors the concept of ReconciliACTION by moving learning outside the four walls of the classroom, fostering an early childhood worldview rooted in interconnectedness, gratitude, and belonging to the Land.
