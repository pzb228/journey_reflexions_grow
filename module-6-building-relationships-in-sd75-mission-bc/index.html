Module - 6 - Building Relationships in SD75       (Mission, BC)

Building Relationships in SD75
Inviting an Elder or Knowledge Keeper to our classroom isn't just a "check-a-box" activity on the curriculum; it's opening the door to a living relationship. After reviewing this week's readings and analyzing the reality of our district (SD75, Mission), I'm left with several key reflections:

Real vs. Theoretical Accessibility: While the Siwal Si'wes department does incredible work and has guidelines available, just having a PDF stored online isn't enough. We need these protocols to be lived and actively shared during Professional Development Days (Pro-D Days), ensuring that both new and substitute teachers know how to approach them respectfully.

Humanizing the Protocol: It's vital that the district's administrative processes adapt to the warmth of the cultural protocol. This involves ensuring that compensation and recognition for the Elders' time are handled efficiently and promptly, and that the document details the local specificities of the Stó:lō people, such as the proper use of ceremonial tobacco and the logistics to ensure their comfort and well-being during the visit.

Beyond the Classroom: As an educator, I know I'm not alone in this. We have important allies: from the Mission Friendship Centre to connect with the urban Indigenous community, to the education offices of local bands like Leq'á:mel. Furthermore, collaborating with local land and salmon stewards in Mission allows us to co-design land-based learning that truly honors traditional environmental stewardship.

Ultimately, protocol is not a rule to be followed, but rather the foundation for building genuine relationships based on respect, reciprocity, and cultural humility.
