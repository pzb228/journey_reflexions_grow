Module - 4 - Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women, Girls, and Two-Spirit (MMIWG2S)

This topic helped me see history from a different perspective, moving beyond simply associating the issue with violence and the statistics of murdered women and girls. While that data is important, it's crucial to highlight that Indigenous women are so much more than that—they are leaders, protectors, artists, mothers, knowledge keepers, and vital voices in their communities.

Something that really resonated with me was the connection between violence against Indigenous women and violence against the land. Colonization has damaged both people and the environment, focusing more on controlling resources than on establishing respectful relationships.

Tamara Bernard's talk deeply impacted me, and I found it beautiful because she emphasized shifting how Indigenous women are represented. The media often focuses solely on tragedy and suffering. But we must also make room for stories of resilience, strength, joy, leadership, and survival. This is powerful evidence of their strength and ongoing journey.

As an educator, I believe that sensitive topics like MMIWG2S must be approached with great care and empathy. We must strive to create emotionally safe classrooms where these conversations can take place without resorting to sensationalism, but rather with a critical, empathetic, and above all, respectful approach. Bringing Indigenous voices into the classroom and listening directly to their experiences is fundamental for mutual healing.

In conclusion, this topic reminded me that education is not just about teaching facts, but about helping students develop genuine empathy, awareness, and respect for human dignity.
