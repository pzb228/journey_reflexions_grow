Module - 7 - Reflection

Selected Artifacts
- What Is Reconciliation? & Reconciliation: What Does It Mean?
- Courage: Going Forward in Aboriginal Education (Brad Baker)
- UNDRIP and the DRIPPA Action Plan
- Two-Eyed Seeing and the Educational Calls to Action

Reflection & Application to Practice

Reviewing these resources helped me understand that reconciliation is not a checklist or a one-time activity; it is an ongoing commitment to building respectful relationships and understanding both past and present realities. As an educator in training, I see clearly that our role in learning spaces is to open doors so that Indigenous voices, histories, and ways of knowing hold the place they rightfully deserve.

Three key insights stood out to me:

1. The courage to change: Brad Baker’s presentation deeply resonated with me. It made me think about the courage needed to challenge deeply rooted colonial practices, and it reminded me of the importance of personal reflection before taking professional action.

2. Legal backing: Reading about UNDRIP and the DRIPPA Action Plan opened my eyes to something fundamental: Indigenous rights are not just good intentions or ideals; they are backed by solid legal frameworks. This reinforces our responsibility to ensure that families and communities are genuinely respected and heard within the school environment.

3. Combining perspectives: The concept of Two-Eyed Seeing shifted my perspective. It is not about choosing between Indigenous and Western knowledge or making them compete, but about seeing them as complementary ways of learning. It challenges me to think critically about what kind of knowledge we are prioritizing in the classroom.

Connection to Inquiry Project & Next Steps

This module connects directly to my Inquiry Project. Throughout this process, I have been exploring authentic ways to integrate Indigenous perspectives into learning. Now I know for certain that true integration is not just about adding a topic to a lesson plan to check a box; it is about building real relationships based on mutual respect and reciprocity.

As I continue my training and apply this to my practice, I commit to looking for natural ways to integrate these perspectives, creating spaces where all students feel included, and keeping up my own learning about truth and reconciliation.
