My Thoughts on Prejudices and Teaching

We all have prejudices and ideas shaped by our upbringing or life experiences. It’s funny how even within the same family, personalities can be completely different just because we had different friends or grew up at different times. These biases radically change how we perceive life. As educators, we need to recognize that just like all the fingers on our hands are different, this diversity is even bigger among human beings. That’s why we are called to be inclusive and understanding.

Understanding Our Biases

  • Explicit Prejudice: When it is done consciously.

  • Implicit Prejudice: This one can exist silently in our thoughts and reactions without us even realizing it.

When we talk about education, even the smallest assumptions can deeply affect a student's school experience and their sense of belonging.

In the Classroom

It is so important to reflect on those moments in the classroom where we might misinterpret a student's participation or behavior. These readings reminded me that children express themselves in so many different ways, and as educators, we must approach learning with empathy, curiosity, and a lot of humility.

Moving Forward

The learning process is never-ending—I find myself repeating this more and more every day. This is a continuous journey. Recognizing our own biases isn't about feeling bad; it’s an act of responsibility. My goal is to keep creating spaces where students feel valued, respected, and safe to just be themselves.

A Personal Reflection

Looking back at my own experience, I realize how much graphic representation and media shape how we see other cultures. Growing up, so many stereotypes were just normalized through TV and school. (Honestly, I didn’t realize it back then, but doing this activity brought back so many memories).

At the same time, seeing public figures like Sofía Vergara openly celebrating her roots reminds me how important cultural pride and positive representation really are. However, Indigenous peoples have often been represented through incomplete or even harmful narratives, minimizing their value. This module really made me reflect on where my own assumptions come from, and the importance of continuing to listen, learn, and unlearn.

"Recognizing biases doesn’t imply guilt, but rather growth, awareness, and responsibility."
