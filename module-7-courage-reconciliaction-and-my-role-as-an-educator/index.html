Module - 7 - Courage, ReconciliACTION, and My Role as an Educator

1. Role Models and Mentors
In his TEDx talk, Brad Baker emphasizes that "it will take courage for us to be successful in education" and to change the system. This really made me think about my own journey:

- My Role Models: My greatest role models are those educators who prove that every child can succeed when they feel valued and supported. I truly believe that meaningful learning only happens when we build strong, caring relationships with students and their families.

- My Mentors: As I work to integrate Indigenous perspectives, my essential mentors are local Elders, Knowledge Keepers, and Indigenous educators. Listening to their voices is the only way to teach with real authenticity and respect.

2. Living ReconciliACTION
Roberta Jamieson notes that “Reconciliation is not just a noun... it is also a verb.” She calls this ReconciliACTION, which resonates deeply with my vision as a teacher:

What it means to me: It means reconciliation isn't a one-time event or a textbook chapter; it is a daily practice. It means acknowledging the truth of Canada's past and making intentional choices every day to create equity and belonging in my classroom.

Fostering Allyship: I can share this mindset with my students by encouraging empathy, curiosity, and critical thinking. By bringing Indigenous stories and local community connections into the classroom, I want to teach youth that being an ally is an active commitment.

3. Walking the Path in My District
Investigating the work in the Mission Public School District (SD75) gives me a lot of hope. The district is actively walking the path toward an inclusive, antiracist system by:

Recognizing and honoring local Stó:lō territories.

Collaborating genuinely with local Indigenous communities.

Implementing the First Peoples Principles of Learning to ensure education is holistic and relational.

Key Takeaway
Ultimately, this reflection reminds me that building a just school system is an ongoing journey. I choose to embrace the courage Brad Baker speaks of to keep challenging my own assumptions, and the commitment to live reconciliation as the active verb Roberta Jamieson described.
