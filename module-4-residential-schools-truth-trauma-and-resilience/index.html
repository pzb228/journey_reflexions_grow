Module -4 - Residential Schools: Truth, Trauma, and Resilience

Although I already knew a bit about colonization and residential schools before this module, these resources helped me understand the emotional and intergenerational impact on a much deeper level. What struck me the most was realizing how Indigenous children were stripped of their families, languages, traditions, and identities, all under the guise of "education."

The video Off to School was especially uncomfortable to watch because it presented residential schools as something beautiful and beneficial. It is a clear example of how media and institutions normalized assimilation and hid the true suffering of Indigenous children. Watching it today made me reflect on how dangerous it is when only one perspective is told. I've always believed that every story has at least two sides, and when we only focus on one, we risk believing a completely distorted narrative.
Another major theme throughout the videos was the loss of identity. Many children were punished just for speaking their language or expressing their culture. Yet, alongside the trauma, these stories also showed incredible resilience and survival. Despite everything, Indigenous communities have kept fighting to protect their traditions, histories, spirituality, and cultures.

Senator Murray Sinclair's words deeply affected me. He reminded Canadians that residential schools were not just accidental mistakes, but part of a system intentionally designed to erase Indigenous cultures. This really shifted my perspective on education and the massive responsibility schools carry in shaping society.

As an educator, I believe schools must become spaces where Indigenous histories are taught honestly and respectfully. Students need to learn about the injustices of the past, but also about resilience, truth, and reconciliation. Facing these histories can be uncomfortable, but it is absolutely necessary if we want to build truly inclusive and respectful classrooms.
