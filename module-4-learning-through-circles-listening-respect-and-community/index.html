Module - 4 - Learning Through Circles: Listening, Respect, and Community.

These readings helped me associate circles with spaces that have clear intentions, promoting respect and active listening, as well as reflection and the building of meaningful relationships. This last aspect is particularly beautiful; circles are not just spaces for sharing opinions. In a circle, we have the opportunity to look at each other, understand our expressions, and see beyond the words.

Circles create opportunities for each participant to be heard without rigid hierarchical levels, thus fostering more equitable participation. This is what makes the difference compared to other forms of communication, such as debates where the goal is always for one side to be right, or lectures where the objective is usually the unilateral transmission of knowledge.

As an educator, I've enjoyed working with circles before, though, to be honest, not fromthis specific restorative perspective. Drawing from my experience with Montessori pedagogy, I have always considered circles a valuable tool for welcoming activities, group reflections, conflict resolution, and creating spaces where students can respectfully express their emotions, thus developing empathy and a deep sense of belonging.
One of the most important takeaways from these readings is that dialogue circles have deep roots in Indigenous traditions. For this reason, it's crucial to understand Indigenous protocols before implementing a sharing circle. Without this knowledge, there's a risk of using the practice superficially or out of context. Truly understanding its origin and meaning demonstrates respect for Indigenous communities and helps prevent cultural appropriation.

Overall, this learning made me reflect on the importance of creating spaces where students not only learn academic content but also how to listen to and respect one another, building positive and lasting relationships.
