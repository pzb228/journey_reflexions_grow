Modules - 3 & 4 - Reflection

ArtifactsWhat I learnedConnecting with My Teaching Practice and Inquiry Project
Reflection on Biases and TeachingThis activity led me to reflect on how personal experiences and systemic biases influence our worldviews. Acknowledging that these behavioral tendencies exist within all of us—and addressing them from a place of shared humanity—is a significant step toward ensuring they do not impact our pedagogical decisions or the relationships we build with our students.Critically examining my own beliefs to dismantle personal biases is vital to ensuring that every student feels valued. This insight aligns seamlessly with my professional commitment to fostering inclusive and equitable educational environments.
Doctrine of Discovery ReflectionI learned about the historical impact of the Doctrine of Discovery and understood how its effects continue to influence the relationship between Indigenous peoples and government institutions today.This reminded me of how important it is to teach history by looking at different perspectives and listening to the voices of the people who actually experienced it. Doing this helps students develop critical thinking. It also strengthened my commitment to helping with reconciliation and healing through education.
Learning From the Land ReflectionWhen we connect learning with the land and nature, we offer more meaningful experiences that stay in the minds and hearts of our students. This helps them build a deep relationship with the world around them.This reflection inspired me to look for more learning opportunities outside the classroom and to bring in hands-on activities that connect school subjects with the real world. This is also directly related to my interest in bringing Indigenous perspectives into science education.
Indigenous Narratives and Colonization Reflection

I looked at how the main stories we are told have shaped our understanding of history, and how other voices have often been left out. I realized how important it is to listen to and value different kinds of knowledge. This reminded me that every story has more than one side, and we should always listen to both perspectives.

As a teacher, I want to create spaces where different perspectives are recognized and respected. This learning helps my inquiry project by giving me a better understanding of how to include Indigenous ways of knowing in real and meaningful ways.

Final Reflection

These modules helped me see even more clearly that education plays a vital role in building respectful relationships and helping with reconciliation. I learned that teaching is not just about passing down information, but about making knowledge meaningful. This made me reflect on my responsibility as a teacher to notice my own biases and question the main historical stories we are told. Since history is usually written by people in power, we need to create opportunities for students to learn from different points of view.

Regarding my inquiry project, these experiences have strengthened my interest in finding ways to include Indigenous perspectives in my teaching. I truly believe that learning from the land and nature, valuing different kinds of knowledge, and thinking critically about history are key to creating a more inclusive and meaningful education for all students.
