Module - 5- Revitalization of Indigenous Languages

Language plays a vital role in cultural identity and well-being. Through language, people connect with their history, traditions, values, and community. As a Spanish speaker, my language helps me maintain a connection to my cultural roots and allows me to express my thoughts, emotions, and experiences. When my daughter relies solely on English, I try to encourage her by speaking only to her in Spanish so she doesn't lose her identity. Language is much more than a communication tool; it's an important part of who we are.

Reflecting on the revitalization of Indigenous languages, I realize I haven't observed much of it in my immediate neighborhood because I don't usually interact closely with my neighbors due to time constraints. Therefore, I'm not fully aware of the extent to which Indigenous languages ​​are being used or revitalized in my local community.

However, through my work in schools as an educational assistant and my everyday life, I have noticed several significant efforts to honor and promote Indigenous languages ​​and cultures. In some schools, Indigenous words and phrases are displayed on hallway bulletin boards and classroom materials. One practice I find particularly meaningful takes place at a traditional elementary school where, each morning after singing the national anthem, a second-grade class acknowledges the traditional territory in which the school is located using an Indigenous language. Outside of school, I have also noticed that on some major highways, the road signs include Indigenous place names alongside English ones. These daily practices and visual reminders help everyone develop awareness and respect for Indigenous peoples and their connection to the land.

I am also familiar with C̓əsqənelə Elementary School in Maple Ridge. The fact that the school bears an Indigenous name demonstrates a commitment to recognizing and honoring Indigenous language and culture within the education system. Actions like these help make Indigenous languages ​​more visible and valued in everyday life.

I believe more can be done to support the revitalization of Indigenous languages. Schools could increase opportunities for students to learn Indigenous greetings, songs, stories, and place names. Communities could support language classes, cultural events, and partnerships with Indigenous elders and keepers of knowledge. Technology, books, films, and digital resources can also play an important role in making Indigenous languages ​​accessible to younger generations.

Seeing all these efforts also touches me personally—it strengthens my own desire to keep my culture alive. I feel that I was the one who chose to come to Canada, and instead of others adapting to my culture, I can learn and embrace the Indigenous culture of this land without abandoning my roots. Every time I do something related to my culture, I feel like I have a little piece of Colombia with me.

Language protection and education must be prioritized because language carries cultural knowledge, traditions, and ways of understanding the world. Losing a language means losing a piece of human history, while keeping it alive brings deep healing, pride, and emotional well-being to the communities. Supporting Indigenous languages ​​contributes to reconciliation, strengthens cultural identity, and helps ensure that future generations can continue learning about and celebrating Indigenous cultures. Preserving these languages ​​is not only important for Indigenous communities but also for Canadian society as a whole.
